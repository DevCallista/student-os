A study companion for the coming session — Harmattan and Rain semesters, side by side, with free resources for every course.

Two semesters, seventeen courses, one SIWES placement running quietly underneath both. It's a full year — the kind that either builds your foundation for everything after it, or gets survived one deadline at a time. Aim for the first one. None of these courses is out of reach on its own; the difficulty is mostly volume and pacing, and both of those respond well to a plan you actually follow.

This isn't a replacement for your lecturers, your department's course outline, or the handouts you'll be given in class — think of it as the layer underneath: something to turn to when a lecture moves faster than your notes, or when you want a second explanation of something before a test. Use it alongside your coursework, not instead of it.

A note on the resources below: these are free, publicly available e-books, docs, and video courses matched to each course's outline — not official course materials, and not guaranteed to match your lecturer's exact syllabus, emphasis, or assessment style. Cross-check anything high-stakes against your own department's outline before relying on it for an exam.

When official PDFs and course materials become available for any of these courses, the Google Drive link will be shared in the WhatsApp group — this page covers the gap until then.

Strategy

A Reasonable Study Plan

Nothing exotic — a rhythm built to survive a full course load without heroics in exam week. Adjust the specifics, keep the shape.

Every Week, All Year

The weekly rhythm

The single highest-leverage habit for a course load like this is a fixed weekly review slot per course — not more study time overall, just time that isn't allowed to slip. A workable split across a normal week:

  • Same-day pass (20–30 min): the evening of each lecture, skim your notes once and jot down anything that didn't make sense. This is what actually prevents backlog — it's far cheaper than re-learning a topic from scratch a month later.
  • Two deep-work blocks (60–90 min): pick two courses a day, on rotation, so every course gets a real block roughly twice a week. Practical courses (web dev, mobile, database programming, OOP) get their block spent coding, not reading.
  • One weekend consolidation block (2–3 hrs): review the whole week across all courses, redo anything you flagged, and skim ahead at what's coming next week.
Weeks 1–3 of a Semester

Establish, don't sprint

  • Read each course outline against the resource list once, and pick one primary free resource per course rather than several competing ones — switching resources mid-topic wastes more time than it saves.
  • For SIWES II: start your logbook habit from week one. A logbook filled in weekly from day one takes an evening to finish at the end; one reconstructed from memory in month three takes a weekend and is worse.
  • Front-load the heaviest theory courses (Data Structures & Algorithms, Data Analysis, Operating Systems) — they compound, so a shaky foundation here costs you later in the semester more than a shaky foundation in, say, GST.
Middle Weeks

Build the practical courses in public

  • Courses with a PH (practical hours) component — web dev, mobile dev, CMS, database programming, networking — are best learned by building one small real thing, not by finishing a video course passively. Pick a toy project per course and let the free resource guide you through building it.
  • Bring what you build to Forge Labs sessions or study partners; explaining a half-finished project surfaces gaps faster than solo review does.
  • Keep the same-day pass habit running — this is where it's most tempting to drop, and most costly to drop.
2–3 Weeks Before Exams

Shift from learning to retrieval

  • Stop consuming new material in theory-heavy courses and switch to past questions, practice problems, and self-testing — recognizing an answer and producing one under exam conditions are different skills, and only the second one is tested.
  • Group courses by how they're actually examined: courses with heavy calculation (Data Analysis) need timed practice sets; concept-and-definition courses (MIS, Ethics & Legal Issues, GST) respond better to writing short answers from memory and checking them.
  • Revisit your own flagged-confusion notes from the "same-day pass" habit — that list is your highest-yield revision material, because it's already filtered down to what you personally found hard.
Exam Week

Protect sleep over cramming hours

By exam week, the return on an extra hour of review is small next to the cost of a bad night's sleep before a paper. Keep review sessions short and targeted — your own flagged notes and one past-question set per course — and treat rest as part of the preparation, not time stolen from it.

Balancing the Two Semesters

Core vs. elective, Harmattan vs. Rain

In Harmattan, your two electives (Data Compression & Web-Based Multimedia, Management Information Systems) are lower-unit and more reading-based than the core courses around them — it's reasonable to give them a lighter, later-scheduled slot in your week rather than equal time to something like Data Structures & Algorithms. In Rain, there are no electives, but the load is more evenly practical — treat GST 312 and IFT 308 (Ethics & Legal Issues) as the "read and retain" courses you can review in shorter, more frequent bursts, freeing your longer blocks for the build-heavy courses like Web Application Development, Mobile Application Development, and Operating Systems.
